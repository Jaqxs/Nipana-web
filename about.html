01 — Identity

Started small.
Stayed close to the work.

NIPANA Atlas began with one concession and a small warehouse in Misungwi. The shape of the business has stayed the same: a mine, a trade desk, and a yard. Different operations, one standard.

We prefer accuracy over drama. That means visible weighing, clear paperwork, careful stock control, and direct accountability when something needs attention.

What sits under one roof

Mining Gold mining in Geita and Tabora, with the same operating rhythm at each site.
Gold Trading A licensed desk in Mwanza that buys from licensed producers and sells to verified buyers.
Wholesale Supply Cement, steel, pipes, tools and heating goods stocked for sites that need them on time.
How we keep things straight

Checks and balances.

Five people on the board, two of them independent. Real meetings, real minutes, and a Tanzanian auditor every year.

The Board

Five directors, two independent. Quarterly meetings rotate between the office, Dar es Salaam, and working sites.

Audit & Risk

An independent director chairs the committee. Controls are reviewed through the year, and the books are audited annually.

Safety & Community

Incidents, rehabilitation, and village relationships report straight to the board instead of getting lost in the middle.

Compliance

Licences, filings, and customer due diligence are handled before the work moves, not after.

How we grew

One business,
three working parts.

We did not build this by chasing noise. We built it by keeping the mine, the desk, and the yard close enough to manage properly.

Stay close to the ground

Decisions are made where the work happens. The team can see the pit, the stock, and the desk without leaving Mwanza.

Keep one standard

What we weigh, what we buy, and what we sell all follow the same rule: visible, recorded, and easy to check.

Grow in working layers

When one line is steady, we add the next. That is how the mine, the desk, and the yard became one operating shape.

Leave things better

We keep the land, the books, and the relationships in better shape than when we arrived. That is the standard that matters.
